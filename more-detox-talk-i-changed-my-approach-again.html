more detox talk (i changed my approach... again)

It's been 11 days since my last post, and since then I've marked 4 days as failures and 2 days as successes.

4 and 2 don't add up to eleven? Uhm, yeah, they don't. I don't know how to explain what happened without coming off as a hopeless screen addict. Not using YouTube for 30 days shouldn't be that hard otherwise. Maybe I am addicted to tech? If I am, hopefully it's not hopelessly.

Here's an outline of how the six days I did log went:

  • June 5th: Success. I spent most of the day doing stuff I intended to do. Spent time on YouTube during my breaks.
  • June 6th: Failure. Same as June 5th, but I scrolled Reddit during my breaks instead.
  • June 7th: Failure. Binged Rabbit Hole covers.[1]
  • June 8th: Success. Got through the day without using tech for fun.
  • June 9th: Success. Out of the house most of the day.
  • June 10th: Failure. More Rabbit Hole.
  • June 11th: Failure. Binged KATSEYE content on YouTube.

By my standards, only June 8th was a win. As mentioned in my previous post, I've found that defining hard rules that work for me is difficult, if not impossible.

One such rule I thought up was "I can't use YouTube for anything other than studying,". It seemed good enough at first, but in practice, two issues came up. The first was that I found other ways to waste time on the web, outside of the go-to YouTube and Reddit. The second was that cutting off my primary way of winding down after a long, tiring day only made those days even more long and tiring.[2] A day would come where I'd be so fed up with studying that I'd reason myself into a doomingly extensive binge, thinking I "deserved" it.

Also in my previous post, I mentioned using my own judgement to decide whether my screen usage was mostly intentional and useful. Here's that part:

I've kind of flipped now, and mark days as successes if no period of mindless surfing or scrolling happened. As long as my screen usage was vastly intentional and useful, I'm okay with that.

I think it's an idea worth exploring — improving my poor conscious decision-making, instead of totally relying on apps and extensions to suppress it. However, June 5th me didn't change much about her approach to steer things that way. So, naturally, I forgot about it after a few days and reverted to that all-or-nothing pattern.

I've made a few changes now, and think they're a step in the right direction:
  • Instead of writing a single journal entry at the day's start, I've shifted to interstitial journaling.[3] I don't have to write entries in my one paper journal; if I'm at my laptop, I can open Notepad and narrate my thoughts there.[4]
  • I've made my blockers a bit stricter. I've set passwords for them that are impossible to memorise, written them down and put them in a high cupboard far from where I usually am during the day.[5] For desktop browsers, this also meant enforcing the extensions through the registry, so they couldn't be disabled or removed.[6][7]
  • I've eased the amount of studying and work-ish stuff I do each day, so stuff will be a bit slower, but my routine will be more consistent and sustainable in the long term.

That's all. Again, I'm in for the long game with this and expect to make more adjustments as the days go on.

[1]: Yes, the DECO*27 song.

[2]: You could argue that one should have more (particularly analogue) ways to relax or have fun. Full reliance on tech for any purpose should be discouraged, both because you shouldn't be putting all of your eggs into one basket (device?) and that you don't have the same permanence and reliability that you do with not-digital stuff. Phones can shut off and not power on again, files can become corrupted or accidentally deleted and cloud services can raise prices or just cease to exist. Things like that.

I agree, but I can't conjure a tech-free hobby out of thin air. It'll take a bit of time to reroute that desire for dopamine from www.youtube.com to a bookshelf or sketchbook and pencil. I'm hoping this approach allows that to happen.
